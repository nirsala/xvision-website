Indoor vs. Outdoor LED: What Changes When You Go Inside

Indoor LED displays operate in a fundamentally different environment from their outdoor counterparts, and the specifications reflect this. Brightness requirements are lower — typically 800 to 2,500 nits rather than the 5,000–8,000 nits demanded outdoors — which enables finer pixel pitches at lower cost. Pixel pitches of P1.2 to P2.5 are now commercially viable for corporate and retail indoor applications, delivering imagery indistinguishable from a broadcast monitor at viewing distances of 2–5 metres.

Thermal conditions also shift. Israeli commercial interiors are heavily air-conditioned in summer, creating thermal cycling as staff open and close doors. Indoor LED panels need adequate thermal design, but they do not require the aggressive IP-rated enclosures and active ventilation systems mandatory for outdoor units. The result is slimmer, lighter panels that can be flush-mounted, recessed, or suspended without structural complexity.

How to Specify an Indoor LED Display: The Four Critical Decisions

Getting these four decisions right determines whether your indoor LED installation delivers on its promise:

1. Pixel Pitch

Divide the minimum viewing distance (in metres) by 1,000 — that gives you the maximum pixel pitch in millimetres at which the image will appear seamless. A viewer 3 metres from the screen: 3,000 ÷ 1,000 = P3.0 maximum. For a boardroom where executives sit 2 metres from a presentation wall, P2.0 or finer is required. Over-specifying (too fine a pitch) wastes budget without visible quality gain; under-specifying produces a visible dot-matrix appearance at normal viewing distance.

2. Screen Size and Format

Indoor LED panels are modular — they tile seamlessly to any width and height. Standard cabinet sizes of 500×500mm or 1,000×500mm allow custom aspect ratios impossible with fixed-size commercial displays. This matters for Israeli retail environments where shopfront widths rarely conform to standard 16:9 proportions.

3. Content Management System

The CMS is where your investment lives day-to-day. For Israeli businesses, mandatory requirements include: full Hebrew RTL support, cloud-based scheduling (no on-premise server dependency), mobile update capability, and multi-zone layout support for screens running simultaneous news tickers, product information, and branded backgrounds. Pixel by Keshet bundles a cloud CMS with all indoor LED installations.

4. Service and Warranty

LED panels are field-serviceable at module level — individual modules can be replaced without removing the entire screen. A credible Israeli supplier should offer: on-site warranty service, spare module inventory held locally (not overseas), and remote diagnostics with proactive fault detection. Pixel by Keshet operates a spare-parts warehouse in Israel to guarantee next-business-day module replacement across the country.

Total Cost of Ownership: Beyond the Purchase Price

Indoor LED screens have very low running costs compared to traditional display technologies. LED panels consume a fraction of the power of fluorescent-backlit displays of equivalent size, require no lamp replacement, and typically achieve 100,000 hours of operation before brightness drops to 50% of original specification — over eleven years of continuous operation. Factor the purchase price, installation, CMS subscription, power, and maintenance across a seven-year lifecycle, and LED consistently delivers a lower total cost than legacy display alternatives.
